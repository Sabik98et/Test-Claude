# Budget Justification: Computing Costs

The project requests $1,556 per year for computing, with an optional $201 for graphics processing unit (GPU) time and contingency, for a total of $1,757. Claude agents set up, launch and evaluate symbolic regression runs with PySR, an open-source Python library, to discover creep equations. One Claude Max licence at the Ohio State negotiated price of $50 per month ($600 per year) supports one graduate researcher. The runs execute on the Cardinal cluster of the Ohio Supercomputer Center (OSC) at the Ohio academic rate of $0.003 per core-hour. Test runs to date used 32 cores for about 2 hours each, in batches of 10 to 33 runs, and production runs scale to 96 cores and can last several days. We estimate yearly use from three weekly scenarios: 20 off-peak weeks of 640 core-hours (ten 32-core test runs), 24 average weeks of 4,800 core-hours (twenty-five 64-core runs of 3 hours) and 8 peak weeks of 23,040 core-hours (two 96-core production runs of 5 days). These scenarios total 312,320 core-hours, or $937 per year. The 500 GB project storage quota costs $19 per year at $3.20 per terabyte-month, billed on quota rather than use. Optional items are 500 GPU-hours ($45) for work outside PySR, which runs on processor cores, and a 10% contingency ($156) for longer production runs or rate changes. The OSC annual credit of $1,000 per principal investigator could offset part of these costs but is not applied, because the credit is shared across the investigator's projects.
